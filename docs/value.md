# NIR value pillars

A fixed supply does not create value by itself. Durable monetary value requires
several properties to work together:

- **verifiable scarcity:** every node independently enforces the 21 million cap;
- **credible neutrality:** no founder, company, or token holder can rewrite
  balances or unilaterally redefine valid intelligence;
- **self-custody:** users can hold and transfer value without a custodian;
- **durability:** many independent nodes retain and validate the ledger;
- **portability and divisibility:** global transfers and eight decimal places;
- **security:** conservative consensus, post-quantum migration, audits, and
  recoverable threshold vaults;
- **privacy:** selective disclosure without a universal surveillance key;
- **liquidity:** enough users and markets to exchange NIR without extreme price
  impact;
- **utility:** payment for verification, model licensing, evaluation, safety,
  challenges, and other demanded services;
- **social credibility:** years of predictable operation and resistance to
  pressure, capture, fraud, and arbitrary rule changes.

NIR's distinguishing thesis is useful issuance: scarce new units are released
for independently proven technological progress, while unsafe candidates lose
their bonds. This thesis is valuable only if measurement cannot be gamed and
the resulting services are wanted by people and organizations.

The prototype implements parts of scarcity, custody, evaluation, and safety.
It does not yet possess decentralization, production privacy, liquidity,
permissionless networking, a long security history, or broad adoption. Those
properties cannot be declared in documentation; they must emerge from a public
network and sustained use.
